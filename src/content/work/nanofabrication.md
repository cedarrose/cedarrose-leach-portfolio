I was part of the first cohort of the Microelectronics Commons Scholars Program, which launched in summer 2024. The program had two immersive phases: a two-week CA DREAMS Cleanroom Gateway at USC's John O'Brien Nanofabrication Laboratory, where I received hands-on training, followed by a six-week internship.

## Cleanroom Gateway

I learned wafer cleaning and handling, photolithography, deposition, etching, metrology, machine operation and cleanroom safety practices. I was trained and certified in semiconductor nanofabrication processes and safe chemical handling, which prepared me for the internship.

## Internship

My internship began with cleanroom machine calibration. I ran multiple trials of SiO₂ deposition on cleaved silicon wafers to measure and recalibrate the PECVD tool's faulty deposition rate.

I then joined the Biomedical Microsystems Laboratory, which develops micro- and nanotechnologies for biomedical applications. I was fascinated by the fabrication of a novel silicon-polymer neural probe, and I continued that research into the school year (see [the neural probe project](/work/neural-probe/)).

Programs that offer such comprehensive training in this specialized industry are exceptionally rare, and I'm grateful for the knowledge and experience I gained.
