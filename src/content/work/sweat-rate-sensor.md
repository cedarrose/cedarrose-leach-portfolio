## About the paper

Sweat rate reflects key physiological states such as hydration and heat tolerance, and wearable sweat rate sensors could help people monitor their health, train smarter and stay safe at work. Getting there has been hard: manufacturing is intricate, sensor responses are nonlinear, and measuring how far sweat has traveled introduces error.

The paper presents a complete wearable platform: a fully printed, flexible sensor patch, readout electronics and a mobile app for continuous, real-time sweat rate monitoring. The patch is 700 mm² and weighs 380 mg. It's made with direct 3D printing and scalable microfluidic fabrication, and serpentine electrodes measure sweat rate through capacitance as sweat fills microfluidic channels 850 μm wide and 164 μm thick.

## My contribution

I helped visualize and communicate the sensor's design by creating detailed 3D models of it in Fusion 360.

Clear communication is one of the most important parts of any scientific advance, and my hope was to make our lab's sensor easier to understand and appreciate. The models show the sensor's structural layers, design features, materials and how it works. The published figures are below.
